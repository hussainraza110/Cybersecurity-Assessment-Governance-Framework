# Assessment Quality Assurance Methodology

> **Implementation note:** This organization-neutral framework document must be tailored, reviewed, and approved before operational adoption. Replace bracketed fields and validate applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, and sector-specific requirements.

## 1. Purpose

Establishes quality controls for assessment planning, execution, evidence, findings, conclusions, and reporting.

## 2. Applicability and principles

This methodology applies where the assessment objective and scope require it. Apply proportionality, authorization, independence where warranted, evidence traceability, risk-based depth, transparency of assumptions, and explicit disclosure of limitations. Tailor methods to the technology, business process, threat context, and applicable obligations.

## 1. Quality objectives

Assessment quality assurance (QA) ensures work is authorized, sufficiently scoped, technically accurate, evidence-based, reproducible where feasible, internally consistent, and communicated without overstating assurance. QA is distinct from the assessed control's effectiveness.

## 2. QA model and independence

Define preparer, reviewer, approver, and quality owner roles. Review independence should be proportionate to assessment risk, complexity, and intended reliance. The reviewer should not approve their own work where meaningful independent review is required. Disclose unavoidable limitations.

## 3. Planning quality gates

Before execution, verify authorization, scope, objectives, criteria, asset population, dependencies, sampling rationale, methods, safety constraints, evidence plan, stakeholder communication, and assessor competence. Material gaps must be resolved or formally accepted before work proceeds.

## 4. Execution quality checks

Confirm work stayed within scope, methods were appropriate, tools and versions were recorded where material, tests were reproducible, unexpected impacts were escalated, and evidence was collected and protected appropriately. Verify that deviations and limitations were documented contemporaneously.

## 5. Evidence quality

Assess relevance, reliability, authenticity, completeness, currency, provenance, integrity, and sufficiency. Trace each material finding and conclusion to evidence. Check that screenshots, exports, logs, and interview notes include enough context to interpret them. Apply evidence handling requirements and minimize sensitive data.

## 6. Finding quality

Check condition, criterion, cause, consequence, affected scope, validation, severity rationale, recommendation, ownership, and retest criteria. Confirm that facts are distinguished from assumptions and that recommendations are feasible and risk-aligned.

## 7. Reporting quality

Review executive summary, scope, exclusions, methodology, limitations, results, risk ratings, findings, management responses, and appendices. Reconcile totals and references. Ensure conclusions do not exceed evidence or sample coverage.

## 8. Review findings and disposition

Record review comments with severity, owner, resolution, and closure. Critical factual, authorization, evidence, or risk-rating issues must be resolved before report issuance. Accepted residual QA issues require explicit approval and disclosure.

## 9. Metrics and continuous improvement

Track rework, review findings, report cycle time, evidence gaps, reopened findings, rating overrides, stakeholder disputes, and recurring methodology defects. Use trends to improve training, templates, tools, and criteria.

## 10. Records and review

Retain QA plans, checklists, reviewer notes, resolutions, approvals, and final quality sign-off. Review this methodology annually and after significant quality failures or changes in assurance requirements.

## 11. Required outputs and records

- QA plan
- Planning gate checklist
- Evidence sufficiency review
- Finding quality review
- Report review checklist
- QA comment log
- Final quality sign-off
