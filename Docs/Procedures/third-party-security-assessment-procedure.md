# Third Party Security Assessment Procedure

> **Implementation note:** Tailor and approve this organization-neutral procedure before operational use. Define local service levels, retention periods, approval authorities, contacts, and technical standards based on applicable obligations and risk.

## 1. Purpose

Risk-based supplier due diligence, approval, monitoring, reassessment, and termination.

## 2. Scope

Applies to relevant personnel, business units, systems, information assets, service providers, and operational environments. Use with applicable policies and related procedures. Document exclusions and rationale.

## 3. Preconditions and authorization

Before execution, the responsible owner must confirm:
- Activity is within organizational authority and approved scope.
- Business purpose, assets, stakeholders, dependencies, and expected outcomes are known.
- Required approvals and change records are complete.
- Personnel have suitable competence and least-privileged access.
- Availability, safety, privacy, legal, contractual, and evidence requirements are considered.
- Communication, rollback, escalation, and contingency arrangements exist where relevant.

Activities affecting production, safety, regulated information, or third-party services require additional authorization as applicable.

## 4. Roles and responsibilities

- **Business sponsor:** performs or oversees assigned responsibilities and maintains required records.
- **Procurement / vendor management:** performs or oversees assigned responsibilities and maintains required records.
- **Third-party service owner:** performs or oversees assigned responsibilities and maintains required records.
- **Information Security:** performs or oversees assigned responsibilities and maintains required records.
- **Privacy / Legal:** performs or oversees assigned responsibilities and maintains required records.
- **Risk owner:** performs or oversees assigned responsibilities and maintains required records.

Approval and execution should be separated where practicable. The procedure owner must document role assignments and manage conflicts.

## 6. Exceptions, failures, and escalation

Record and promptly escalate deviations, failed activities, unexpected impacts, control failures, and suspected security incidents. Pause work where continuation could increase harm, exceed authorization, or compromise evidence. Security exceptions must follow `security-exception-and-risk-acceptance-procedure.md` where applicable. Incident conditions must be handed to `security-incident-response-procedure.md`.

## 7. Records and evidence

Maintain records appropriate to risk and purpose:
- Request, scope, business justification, and authorization.
- Asset, user, supplier, or process identifiers in scope.
- Execution date/time, personnel, tools, and relevant configuration/version details.
- Decisions, approvals, tests, deviations, failures, and communications.
- Evidence references, integrity protections where warranted, and retention requirements.
- Closure, owner acceptance, residual risk, and follow-up actions.

Handle evidence under `docs/methodology/evidence-handling.md`. Do not collect or retain unnecessary personal, secret, or sensitive data.

## 8. Quality assurance and metrics

Define proportionate quality checks, sampling, independent review, and performance measures. Metrics should measure outcomes and control effectiveness, not activity volume alone. Possible measures include completion within target, overdue actions, failure/rework rate, exception age, coverage, review completion, and recurrence. Document metric definitions, denominators, exclusions, and reporting periods.
